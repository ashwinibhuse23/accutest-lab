Summary

Before a new version of a contraceptive injection can reach women, regulators need proof that it performs like the original. Accutest ran a bioequivalence study comparing depot medroxyprogesterone acetate, subcutaneous (DMPA-SC) with Sayana® in 132 healthy women.

The setup: each woman received a single injection, and blood samples were collected for up to 140 days. Precise dosing and a long sampling schedule were the key challenges.

  • All 132 women enrolled on schedule
  • 129 of 132 women completed the study
  • Study completed within the agreed timeline

Why this study mattered

DMPA-SC is a subcutaneous injection used only to prevent pregnancy. When a new version of an existing product is developed, regulators want proof that it performs the same way in the body as the reference product. This is called bioequivalence. As per the USFDA, two products are considered bioequivalent when they are equal in the rate and extent to which the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) becomes available at the site(s) of drug action.

This study compared the test product (DMPA-SC vials) with the reference product, Sayana®. It also monitored the safety and tolerability of a single subcutaneous dose of each.

Three challenges, and how we solved them

Recruiting 132 eligible women

The challenge

The study needed 132 eligible women, and reaching that number during recruitment was a challenge.

Our approach
  • Targeted recruitment strategies.
  • Continuous screening, so eligible volunteers could be enrolled quickly.
  • All 132 women enrolled on schedule.

Collecting blood samples for 140 days

The challenge

Samples were needed for up to 3,360 hours (Day 140) after dosing, and DMPA has a half-life of 6 weeks, so keeping every collection on schedule took careful planning.

Our approach
  • A detailed sampling schedule covering the full 140 days.
  • Close monitoring of collection timelines so each sample was collected when due.

Getting the injection right, every time

The challenge

Dosing meant weighing the products before and after dosing, drawing the test product into syringes, adjusting the dose accurately and keeping the suspension evenly mixed. The risks were inaccurate dosing, contamination, product loss and an uneven suspension.

Our approach
  • Every dosing step planned and monitored in advance.
  • Trained staff handled weighing, withdrawal, dose adjustment and suspension handling.
  • Procedures to limit contamination and product loss, and keep the suspension homogeneous before injection.
  • One consistent injection site: the abdomen, about 3 inches to the right of the navel, avoiding bony areas and the navel itself.

The result

The study was completed successfully and within the agreed timeline. Participant retention stayed strong throughout, even with sampling that ran for 140 days.

What sponsors can take from this

  • Large female cohorts can be recruited on time with targeted outreach and continuous screening.
  • Long ambulatory sampling works when the schedule is detailed and timelines are actively monitored.
  • Injectable studies need precision. Trained staff and clear dosing procedures protect data quality.

Frequently asked questions

What is a bioequivalence study? +

As per the USFDA, two products are considered bioequivalent when they are equal in the rate and extent to which the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) becomes available at the site(s) of drug action. A bioequivalence (BE) study tests this by comparing how a test product and a reference product behave in the body.

What is DMPA-SC used for? +

DMPA-SC (depot medroxyprogesterone acetate, subcutaneous) is a contraceptive injection. It is indicated only for the prevention of pregnancy.

Why did this study collect blood samples for 140 days? +

DMPA has a half-life of 6 weeks, meaning it takes about that long for the amount in the body to fall by half. Sampling therefore has to run long enough to capture the full profile. In this study, samples were collected up to Day 140 after dosing.

What does “parallel design” mean? +

Each participant receives only one of the two products (test or reference) rather than both in turn. Parallel designs are commonly used for long-acting products.

Can Accutest run long-duration studies with ambulatory sampling? +

Yes. This study included blood sampling up to Day 140, managed through a detailed schedule and close monitoring.
